# Issue #17: Research: encoding hard and soft research constraints

## Question

How can user-stated limits on *what the research may try* be encoded, both hard ("don't test this", "this would probably work but we're avoiding it") and soft ("we'd prefer it to stay usable by downstream method X")?

Look at two layers:
- **BO layer**:
  - hard exclusion by keeping the option out of the search space, versus Optuna feasibility constraints (`trial.set_constraint`);
  - soft preferences as penalties on the objective, versus priors over the search space (πBO, prior-guided TPE), versus cost-aware BO;
  - how each choice interacts with warm start and with reject statistics. For example, a penalized objective changes what "this lever matters" means.
- **Agent layer**:
  - rule- or constitution-style review of proposed hypotheses (Constitutional AI, guard models);
  - how prior agentic research systems restrict scope;
  - how to prevent "constraint laundering", where a prohibited idea comes back under a new name.

Also cover how a discouraged option that wins should be reported: surfaced as a tradeoff rather than silently penalized away. Cite primary sources. Findings feed **Research constraints: prohibited vs discouraged**.

**upadhyan** (2026-09-25T03:27:54Z):

**Resolved.** The full findings, including a table of options × layer and the effects on warm start and Sobol, are in [docs/research/research-constraints.md](https://github.com/upadhyan/bo-autoresearch/blob/research/research-constraints/docs/research/research-constraints.md). The Optuna claims were checked against v5.0.0.

**Gist:** Prohibited limits never reach BO. The reviewer prunes the hypothesis, or the harness removes the lever choice or narrows its bound, so the Sobol box equals the allowed box. Discouraged limits stay out of the objective and the sampler: they lower the hypothesis's priority, are logged as per-trial telemetry, and a discouraged winner is reported as a tradeoff. Laundering is caught by mechanism, not name.

**Key facts / recommended options**
1. **`trial.set_constraint` only flags feasibility after the trial has run.** TPE ranks infeasible trials last and GP multiplies EI by the probability of feasibility, so the sampler learns to avoid a point only by *running* it. That breaks "don't test this".
2. **Optuna can't take a user prior.** TPE's prior is one fixed kernel at the midpoint of the range, and `prior_weight` is deprecated. πBO isn't in Optuna.
3. **πBO can't express a hard limit, and its preference fades.** It requires π > 0 everywhere and decays as β/n, and warm-start seeds inflate n. Cost-aware BO behaves the same way. Both change *where the search samples*, not *what counts as best*.
4. **A penalised objective `f − λg` is the worst option.** It changes the objective (a new run), locks λ across seeds, and inflates the discouraged lever's total-order Sobol index, so the reject statistic would measure the preference itself.
5. **Proposal:** prohibited limits never reach BO. They are handled by pruning the hypothesis or excluding the option from the search space, and seeds outside the allowed space are dropped.
6. **Proposal:** use a pre-check predicate plus `set_constraint` plus a sentinel only as a last resort for prohibited regions that aren't box-shaped. It works with TPE only, and reparameterising into a box is better.
7. **Proposal:** discouraged limits are handled at the agent layer: lower priority and a stated reason. Each trial logs a `compat:<id>` flag per soft limit.
8. **Proposal:** the round summary reports both the best trial overall and the best trial that honours each soft limit, with the gap and its credible interval, and the user decides.
9. **Proposal:** the reviewer is a guard model with the policy in its prompt, returning `{allow|prune|deprioritize, constraint_id, rationale}`. It checks every hard rule on every hypothesis, and the orchestrator can't edit the registry.
10. **Proposal:** catch laundering by mechanism. Hypotheses declare a structured `mechanism` field, and similarity retrieval against the prohibited list and previously pruned hypotheses triggers a strict re-review. A hook scans the lever-code diff, and a runtime assertion checks each trial's resolved config.
