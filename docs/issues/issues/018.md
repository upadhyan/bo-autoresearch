# Issue #18: Directives: prohibited vs discouraged vs preferred

- State: CLOSED
- Author: upadhyan
- Created: 2026-09-25T03:18:31Z
- Labels: wayfinder:grilling


## Question

How does the loop represent and enforce the user's limits on *what may be tried*? This is separate from the objective's feasibility constraints, like runtime < X. Decide:
- **Severity tiers:** prohibited (hard) versus discouraged (allowed but costly), plus whether "preferred" or "downstream-compatibility" wishes are a third tier.
- **Where the limits live:** in the pre-loop grilling output, the hypothesis registry, or both.
- **The constraint reviewer subagent:** what it checks each hypothesis against, whether it can veto (prohibited) or only reprioritize (discouraged), and when it runs. Candidates are at hypothesis registration and before each round.
- **BO-level representation:** is a discouraged lever or value excluded, penalized, or given a low prior?
- **Reporting:** how a discouraged option that wins is reported to the user.
- **Staleness:** whether and how the user can revise constraints mid-research.
- **Naming:** "constraint" already means the objective's feasibility constraints, so pick a distinct term.

## Resolution

**Gist:** there are two severity tiers, **prohibited** and **discouraged**, kept in a directive registry that the harness owns and that pre-loop grilling writes.
- **Prohibited** limits never reach BO. They prune a hypothesis or cut values out of its levers' ranges, and the harness checks them again at registration, on every commit and before every trial.
- **Discouraged** limits never touch the objective or the sampler. They lower priority, are flagged on each trial, and a winning discouraged option is reported as a confirmed tradeoff.
- **Fit with the user's intent** is a separate judgement, not a tier. The registration reviewer checks each hypothesis against a **research brief** and may lower its priority or park it, but never prune it.

This builds on [Research: encoding hard and soft research constraints](https://github.com/upadhyan/bo-autoresearch/issues/17).

### Tiers
1. **Only two tiers.** A "preferred" wish behaves exactly like a discouraged one, so pre-loop grilling rewrites it as a discouraged directive, e.g. "prefer ONNX-exportable" becomes "discouraged: ops that can't be exported to ONNX".

### Directive registry
2. **The harness owns the registry.** Pre-loop grilling writes it, it is stored with the experiment log, and it is a protected path the orchestrator can't edit. Each directive has:
   - an id, a severity, a statement and a reason;
   - a scope: a mechanism, particular lever values, or a resource;
   - optional machine checks: a config predicate and forbidden code patterns.

   Hypotheses declare which directive ids they touch. Protected paths are a separate kind of entry in the same registry.

### Reviewer
3. **The registration reviewer checks directives, conflicts and fit with intent.**
   - **When it runs:** at registration, at revival, when a parked hypothesis returns, and on every live and pruned hypothesis whenever the registry is revised.
   - **Directive verdict:** `{allow | prune | deprioritize, directive_id, rationale}`, schema-checked by the `SubagentStop` hook. A deprioritize verdict needs the orchestrator to supply a stated reason for testing the hypothesis.
   - **Forcing:** the harness `register` command refuses any hypothesis that has no reviewer record.

### Prohibited: enforcement
4. **Values.**
   - Lever ranges and categorical options must lie inside the allowed region, or registration is refused.
   - Seeds outside the allowed region are dropped.
   - Allowed regions that aren't box-shaped are reparameterised into a box. The predicate plus `set_constraint` sentinel is a last resort.
5. **Before every trial**, the harness checks the resolved config against every prohibited predicate and refuses the trial if one fails.
6. **Catching workarounds**, by mechanism rather than by name:
   - a structured `mechanism` field on each hypothesis;
   - similarity search against prohibited directives and previously pruned hypotheses, which triggers a strict re-review;
   - a harness scan of every commit's diff for forbidden code patterns, which refuses the commit on a match;
   - the per-trial check from item 5.

### Discouraged: handling
7. **Discouraged values stay in the search space on equal footing**: no penalty, no prior and no exclusion. The only effects are:
   - lower priority for discouraged hypotheses;
   - a `compat:<id>` flag on each trial, computed from its resolved config so it can be recomputed later.
8. **A discouraged option that wins.**
   - Round summaries and the final deliverable report the best overall configuration and the best configuration that honours each directive, with the gap and its credible interval.
   - The final confirmation, paid for from the reserve, also runs the best honouring configuration.
   - The user chooses. The distilled branch uses the overall best, and the honouring configuration is delivered as a lever-value override.

### Revising directives
9. **Revisions happen only at a checkpoint or during a user pause**, take effect at the next round boundary, and are logged with a new version number.
   - **A new or tightened prohibition:** registered hypotheses that violate it are pruned, and active ones are parked. Trials that violate it become ineligible, which counts as a search-space change and ends the round.
   - **A relaxed prohibition:** pruned hypotheses become proposed again and are re-reviewed.
   - **A discouraged change:** priorities and flags are recomputed.

### Intent fit (judged, not a tier)
10. **Pre-loop grilling writes a research brief** into the registry. It covers:
    - the purpose, e.g. "speed up the existing implementation without changing behaviour", "a publishable contribution" or "a production model";
    - what counts as in scope, and what counts as a contribution;
    - a complexity appetite;
    - provenance rules, e.g. "borrowing published components would undercut the contribution".

    Each hypothesis declares its `provenance`: novel, adapted from a cited source, or a standard technique.
11. **The intent verdict** is given by the same reviewer, at the same points:
    - **fits**;
    - **stretch**: lower priority, with a stated reason;
    - **off-intent**: **parked** with the reason "off-intent: …".

    Taste never prunes. Pruning is reserved for prohibited directives, which the user states explicitly. Parking keeps off-intent hypotheses from using up budget under the exhaustion rule, and the user can bring them back at a checkpoint.
12. **Proposed directives.** When two or more hypotheses are parked for the same intent concern, the round summary proposes a directive to the user. The reviewer never writes directives itself.

### Glossary
- **Directive** now has two severities, prohibited and discouraged. Preferences are written as discouraged directives.
- Added **Research brief**.

### Knock-on for open tickets
- **Experiment log schema:** add the directive registry (versioned, with protected paths and the research brief), each hypothesis's `mechanism`, `provenance` and directive ids, reviewer records (directive and intent verdicts with rationales), per-trial `compat:<id>` flags, the per-trial prohibited-check outcome, revision events and their effects, and proposed directives.
- **Agent roster and contracts:** the registration reviewer's input (hypothesis, registry, brief, pruned and parked history) and output schema (directive verdict, intent verdict and conflict class). It needs strict re-review on similarity hits. The orchestrator must supply a reason for deprioritized or stretch hypotheses, and proposers must declare `mechanism` and `provenance`.
- **Pre-loop grilling script:** collect directives with their severity, reason and optional predicates and patterns; rewrite preferences as discouraged; and collect the research brief (purpose, scope and contribution, complexity appetite, provenance rules).
- **Runner harness API:** add a refusal to register without a reviewer record, range checks against the allowed region, dropping out-of-region seeds, the per-trial prohibited check, `compat` flags, applying revisions at round boundaries, and confirming the honouring configuration at wrap-up.
- **Enforcement hooks:** add the `SubagentStop` schema check for the reviewer, the diff scan for forbidden patterns at commit, and protection of the registry and brief.
- **Final deliverable:** report the best honouring configuration for each discouraged directive, with the gap and the override. List off-intent parked hypotheses with their reasons, and any proposed directives.
